Why we are running this study

Thank you very much for your time and for taking part in this study with your child!

We are running this study because we want to understand how young children think about situations in which what they want conflicts with what their parent wants. Prior research in child psychology has shown that young children understand that it is wrong to act on a desire that conflicts with a clear moral rule — for example, to push another child off a swing in order to use it oneself. Many of the moral decisions people face in everyday life, however, cannot be guided by such rules. For example, many things children want to do at home are not wrong in themselves, yet they may conflict with what parents or other family members are trying to accomplish. In such cases, do young children think it is wrong to persist with the desired activity? Do their answers depend on whether they understand *why* the activity is disruptive to the parent?

In this study, we asked your child to think about an activity — playing with an attractive object — that is understandably desirable and not inherently wrong, but is nevertheless unwelcome to the parent. We want to know whether children of different ages think it is wrong to act on such a desire when a parent asks them not to, and whether they think doing so feels at least somewhat bad. We are especially interested in whether children think it is worse — and feels worse — to ignore a parent’s request to stop when the parent explains why the activity would interfere with their goal, compared to when the parent gives no explanation. For this reason, in one of the stories your child heard, the mother explained why she was asking the child not to play with the object, while in the other story, she did not.

Request for feedback

We are very grateful that you and your child were among the study’s first participants! Your child’s responses and behavior will give us invaluable clues about what we should change or improve for future participants.

There are some things, however, that only you can tell us because you know your child best. If you’d like, please let us know in the text box below:

  • How easy or difficult was it for your child to do this study?
  • Was there anything that was unclear or may have interfered with your child’s ability to understand the stories or answer the questions truthfully?
